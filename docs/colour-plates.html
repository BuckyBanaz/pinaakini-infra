Pinaakini Infra · website colour direction

Four colour plates for the Pinaakini website

The current site uses black, ivory and gold. Almost every premium developer in India uses that combination, so the site reads as "another luxury template". Below are two palettes already written into the project briefs and two new ones built from Pinaakini's own story. Each plate shows its colours and a small sample of the website in that palette.

Why it feels commonBlack and gold with a thin serif is the default look for luxury real estate. It signals "premium" but not "Pinaakini".
What makes a brand look its ownA colour that comes from the company's story, its own logo, and real photographs of its land and homes.
What changes on the siteColours live in one token file, so switching the whole site to any plate takes under an hour.

A · Forest & Sage

From the frontend brief · recommended

Pinaakini sells land: plots, layouts and farmland around Nellore, a district known for its paddy fields. Deep forest green says land and growth, and very few developers use it, so it stands apart immediately. Champagne stays as a small accent, so it still reads premium.

B · Pinakini River

New · built from the name

Pinakini is the old name of the Penna, the river Nellore stands on. This plate takes its colours from it: river slate for the deep tone, riverbank sand for highlights, and the red laterite soil of the region as one strong accent for links and buttons. It is the most "only Pinaakini" option.

What to do next

  1. Ask the client to pick one plate, or shortlist twoA (Forest & Sage) is already in the brief and fits a land developer. B (Pinakini River) is the most distinctive.
  2. Apply it across the whole siteColours and fonts change in one place, then every page follows. The client then reviews the live site, not a picture.
  3. Replace the borrowed imageryThe same villa render and stock paddy photos make any palette feel generic. Real photos and drone video of Pinaakini's own layouts and homes will do more than any colour.
  4. Add the official logoThe site still shows the name in plain type. A real logo, used in the header, footer and favicon, makes it a brand.
  5. Give the site one signature ideaFor example, a river line that runs through the pages for the Pinakini name, or an interactive layout plan where visitors tap a plot to see its size and status.

Questions for the client

  • Do you already use colours on brochures, hoardings or the office board? If yes, those should guide the choice.
  • Can you share the logo files (SVG or high-resolution PNG)?
  • Do you have real photos or drone videos of your projects?
  • Which numbers can we publish (acres developed, families, projects delivered)?
  • Can you share two or three genuine client testimonials, with permission?
